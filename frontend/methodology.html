Our philosophy

Machine learning in the service of the analyst

MM.AI does not replace the expert eye — it augments it. At its core is a machine learning pipeline that ingests over 150 000 fights across 30+ years, extracts 30+ features per fighter, and trains on the full historical record to produce ratings and predictions that no human analyst could compute manually. We believe the strongest read on a fight emerges from the alliance of algorithmic rigour and human judgement: the machine never forgets a result, weighs thousands of careers without bias, and converges to a stable rating through iterative learning. That gives the human analyst the best possible starting point.

▶

Iterative convergence

Ratings are not computed once — the algorithm runs multiple passes over the entire fight history until all values stabilise. This fixed-point approach resolves circular dependencies (A beats B who beats C who beats A) through repeated learning, typically converging in 5–12 iterations.

◎

Explainable by design

No black box. Every output is traceable: an Elo update, a weighted feature combination, a logistic output. We favour a model a human can understand and challenge over one nobody can audit — every number has a formula behind it.

⚠

Known limits

MM.AI does not model extra-sporting factors — weight cuts, injury, motivation, camp changes. These can decide a night and remain the expert's call. The model is a prior, not a verdict.

01

Iterative Elo — fixed-point learning

Inspired by chess Elo and adapted to MMA, our algorithm replays all 151 538 fights in chronological order, updating every fighter's rating after each bout.

Rating ← Rating + K · ( actual − expected )
K adapts to experience weighted by finish quality bounded to prevent drift

Because each fighter's rating depends on their opponents' ratings, the entire pass is repeated 5–12 times until the maximum per-fighter change drops below 0.5 — a fixed-point convergence that self-corrects circular dependencies across the full graph of 11 000+ fighters.

  • Seeded from external rating points (log-transform calibration)
  • Per-division rescaling to [600 – 2800] after convergence
  • Opponent tier fallback for 32% of bouts with unknown opponents
02

Scouter Rating — composite ML score

A single 0–100 score aggregating four independent feature groups, each capturing a different dimension of fighter quality.

35% Elo 30% Career 20% External 15% Skills
  • Elo component — base_elo normalised to 0-100 per division
  • Career component — win %, finish %, title reigns, title defenses, streak
  • External component — aggregated public rating metrics and quality %
  • Skills component — striking accuracy, grappling, takedown defense (UFC Stats)
All four components are normalised and min-max scaled before weighting, ensuring no single source dominates due to scale differences.
03

Feature engineering

Each fighter is represented as a 30+ dimensional feature vector extracted from their full fight history and multi-source profile.

Ratings
Elo & composite quality scores
Form & momentum
recency-weighted recent results
Career
win & finish rates, title history
Physical
age, height, reach, stance
Striking / grappling
accuracy & defensive metrics

Features are recomputed at every scrape cycle. The recent_form_score applies exponentially increasing weights to the last 5 fights, making the model responsive to momentum without overfitting on a single result.

04

Data sources — multi-source fusion

Four independent sources are cross-referenced per fighter to eliminate single-platform bias and maximise coverage.

External rating DB
Career rating points, quality performance %, Big League Record — authoritative across all promotions since 1993
Official UFC
Division rankings, P4P list, champion status, UFC-only record, nationality, stance
UFC Stats
Per-fight striking, grappling, control time, takedown defense — granular per-round breakdown
Tapology
Full fight history with dates, methods, opponent records at fight time — used for form and chronological Elo replay

Anti-duplicate matching normalises names (accents, apostrophes) and cross-references date of birth to merge profiles across sources without creating ghost fighters.

05

ML prediction engine — comparing two fighters

Given fighters A and B, MM.AI computes a dynamic Elo for each by applying four real-time modifiers to their base rating, then feeds the difference into a logistic regression to output a win probability.

Step 1 — Dynamic Elo (live modifiers)

dyn Elo = base Elo × live modifiers
Age curve Recent form Opposition strength Inactivity decay

Step 2 — Logistic prediction

P(A wins) = σ ( Σ wᵢ · Δfeatureᵢ )
logistic on feature gaps weights learned, not hand-set

The predicted win method (KO/TKO, submission, decision) is derived from the winner's historical finish distribution weighted by their opponent's defensive stats. A high finish rate against a poor grappling defender raises the submission probability, etc.
