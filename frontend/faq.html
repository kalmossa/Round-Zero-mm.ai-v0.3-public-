Frequently asked questions

The questions we get most often about how MM.AI works — and what it can and can't do. Click a question to reveal the answer.

What is MM.AI?

An independent MMA analytics platform that rates fighters and estimates fight outcomes from their full career record across all major promotions (UFC, PFL, Bellator, ONE and regional circuits). It is a final-year student project (TFE) — not affiliated with the UFC or any promotion.

How does MM.AI predict a winner?

Each fighter gets a fight score built from their MM.AI rating, win/loss record, official ranking, recent form, age and activity. The two scores are converted into win probabilities. It is a transparent, explainable heuristic — every term can be traced back to real data — not a black box.

Does MM.AI use AI or deep learning?

No deep learning, no neural network. MM.AI is built on an Elo-style rating (like chess) plus weighted career statistics. We chose explainability on purpose: every number can be justified to a human, which matters far more than a black-box score that nobody can audit.

Why is the favorite sometimes only 55%?

Because the two fighters are close in level. A probability near 50/50 is the model telling you the fight is genuinely competitive — not that it "doesn't know". A high percentage means a clear gap on paper, but it is never a guarantee. MMA is the sport of the upset.

Can I compare fighters from different weight classes?

Up to 2 divisions apart, yes — but treat it as indicative. Ratings converge within a division, so cross-division numbers (the Elo in particular) are not perfectly comparable. At 3 or more divisions apart the comparison and the prediction are disabled.

Why does MM.AI judge a whole career instead of one fight?

One bad night, a flash KO or a controversial decision is noise. What matters is the signal across dozens of fights. A fighter's true level shows up over 50 or 100 bouts, not over the last one or two — so read the trend, not the most recent data point.

Does MM.AI account for injuries, weight cuts or mindset?

Not yet. MM.AI does not model extra-sporting or mental factors — a rough weight cut, personal turmoil, motivation, a camp change or a hidden injury. These can decide a fight and remain, for now, the domain of the human expert. That is exactly why we see MM.AI as a partner to human judgement, not a replacement.

How often is the data updated?

Fighters and events are scraped from several public sources and the ratings are recomputed in batches. The database currently holds well over 10,000 fighters across the UFC, PFL, Bellator, ONE and regional promotions, with new events added as they happen.

A fighter is missing or has wrong data — what can I do?

Multi-source scraping inevitably leaves gaps, especially for regional records. Tell us using the button below and we will look into it — corrections directly improve the ratings.

How accurate is it? Should I bet on it?

MM.AI is a decision-support tool, not an oracle. It is a demonstration model with calibration still in progress. Use it to inform and challenge your own analysis — never as a betting guarantee.

What promotions does MM.AI cover?

MM.AI covers the UFC, PFL, Bellator, ONE Championship and hundreds of regional promotions worldwide. The database currently indexes over 10,000 fighters. Coverage is densest at the top and grows continuously as new fighters are scraped from multiple public sources.

What is the MM.AI Rating?

The MM.AI Rating (0–100) is our composite score: 35% Elo rating (career-long fight-by-fight progression), 30% career statistics (record, finish rate, activity), 20% external ratings and 15% technical skills (striking, grappling). It is computed on-demand — never stale.

What does "recent form" mean?

Recent form is a weighted score of a fighter's last 5 results. The most recent fight counts most, the oldest least. A fighter on a 5-fight win streak scores near 1.0; one on a losing streak scores near 0. It feeds into the fight prediction alongside the overall rating.
